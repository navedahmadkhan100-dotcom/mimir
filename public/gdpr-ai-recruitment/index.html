Mimir / GDPR and Recruitment AI
PRIVACY BY DESIGN

GDPR-aware recruitment AI starts by asking which candidate data the evaluation actually needs.

Mimir is designed around data minimisation: a technical capability assessment usually does not need a candidate’s name, email, phone number or social profile. Basic direct identifiers are therefore reduced in the browser before the prepared candidate evidence is evaluated.

Data minimisation in recruitment

The GDPR principle of data minimisation requires personal data to be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for the stated purpose. For capability evaluation, identity fields are often not necessary to determine whether the CV evidences technologies, project ownership, certifications or delivery scope.

What Mimir currently does

  • Raw uploaded CV files are prepared in the browser rather than being uploaded as raw documents to the evaluation API.
  • Basic direct identifiers such as candidate contact details are redacted locally where detected.
  • The server applies deterministic text masking again as defence in depth.
  • The current Mimir deployment does not use a server database for storing raw candidate CVs.
  • Recruiter decisions remain human-controlled.

What this does not mean

Mimir does not claim legal GDPR compliance merely because these technical controls exist. A production deployment still requires the controller or employer to establish lawful basis, transparency information, processor arrangements, retention and deletion rules, data-subject rights processes, security measures, transfer safeguards where relevant, and a DPIA when required.

Privacy engineering supports compliance; it does not replace the legal and organisational obligations of the organisation using the system.
