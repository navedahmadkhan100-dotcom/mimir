EVIDENCE-BASED RECRUITMENT

Recruitment decisions are stronger when every important claim can be traced to evidence.

A CV is a collection of claims, not a database of verified facts. Evidence-based recruitment means treating those claims according to the strength of the supporting material and keeping uncertainty visible until a recruiter or interviewer resolves it.

Presence is not proof

Keyword matching can find technologies, titles and certifications, but it cannot by itself establish ownership, depth or outcome. “Worked with Azure” and “designed the Azure landing zone” are not equivalent claims. Mimir is built to preserve that difference.

Evidence states instead of forced certainty

Candidate evidence can be direct, partial, contextual, ambiguous or simply not evidenced. Not evidenced is different from contradicted: a CV may omit a detail that is true in reality. That is why Mimir can generate a verification question rather than turning every omission into a failure.

Visual evidence has boundaries too

An architecture diagram can establish that a technical design is documented in the CV, but the picture alone does not necessarily prove that the candidate personally authored it. Text ownership statements and surrounding role evidence still matter.

Auditability for recruiters

Evidence Graph links, claim assessments and deterministic score lineage make it possible to inspect where a recommendation came from. The goal is not to remove recruiter judgement but to make repetitive evidence checking faster and more consistent.
