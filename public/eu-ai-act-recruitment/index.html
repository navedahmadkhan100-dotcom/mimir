Mimir / EU AI Act and Recruitment
RECRUITMENT AI GOVERNANCE

Recruitment AI needs more than a match score: it needs traceability, boundaries and human oversight.

The EU AI Act lists AI systems intended for recruitment or selection—including analysing and filtering job applications and evaluating candidates—within Annex III. Classification still depends on the Regulation’s rules and any applicable exceptions, so a specific deployment needs legal assessment.

Why recruitment AI receives special scrutiny

Employment decisions can materially affect access to work and other fundamental interests. The EU AI Act therefore places recruitment and candidate-evaluation use cases within the high-risk framework in Annex III, subject to Article 6 and its classification conditions.

How Mimir's architecture supports stronger governance

  • Human oversight: Mimir is decision support, not an autonomous rejection engine.
  • Traceability: requirement claims connect to CV evidence and score lineage.
  • Controlled numerical scoring: the language model interprets evidence but does not directly choose the final score.
  • Uncertainty: unsupported claims can become verification questions rather than hidden assumptions.
  • Adversarial review: Odin challenges over-reaching evidence interpretations before scoring.
  • Version metadata: model, prompt, policy and scoring versions are carried in the evaluation audit data.

Readiness is not certification

Mimir does not claim blanket EU AI Act compliance. Whether a deployment is high-risk, which actor obligations apply, and what conformity, registration, risk-management, logging, data-governance, transparency, human-oversight and post-market obligations are required depends on the actual role and deployment context.
